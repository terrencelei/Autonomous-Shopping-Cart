How it works

See. Think. Move.

Three jobs, three chips, one loop that repeats thirty times every second.

01 · SEE

A camera with a brain of its own

The camera chip itself runs a neural network thirty times a second, spotting every person in view. The cart locks onto its shopper and memorises the colours they're wearing — so it can tell you apart from a stranger in the same aisle.

02 · THINK

Two smooth decisions per frame

A Raspberry Pi turns "where are you" into "how fast" and "which way". Both are continuous, gentle adjustments — the way you'd ease a car up to a red light — never a lurch between stop and go.

03 · MOVE

Wheels that report back

A small ESP32 chip powers the two wheels, and sensors on each wheel report exactly how far they really turned. The cart constantly compares "what I asked for" with "what actually happened".

Abilities

Small cart. Big manners.

Everything below runs together, live, with no human input after launch.

Keeps a polite distance

It hangs back two metres. Walk on and it follows; stop and it eases to a halt. No tailgating, no bumper taps.

Knows who it's following

Your colour profile is its memory of you. Everyone else in the aisle is just labelled "obstacle" — politely avoided, never followed.

Waits for people in the way

If someone steps between you and the cart, it pauses — still aiming at you — and rolls on the moment the path clears.

Never gives up the search

Lost you around a corner? It turns toward where it last saw you and keeps scanning — patiently, forever — until you're back in view.

Finds its own way back

Turn it around 180° while it's parked and it takes the hint: it retraces its own wheel history and drives back to where it started.

Teaches itself to drive

Every stop teaches it how far it coasts; every turn teaches it how it swings. It tunes its own physics as it goes — no calibration sessions.

Under the hood

Four parts, honestly wired.

Off-the-shelf hardware, custom firmware and control software — all open on GitHub.

📷

Sony IMX500 AI camera

Runs the people-spotting neural network on the image sensor itself, so detection costs the main computer nothing.

🧠

Raspberry Pi 5

The brain. Tracks the shopper, runs both control loops, watches for obstacles, and keeps the map of its own movement.

⚡

ESP32 + dual motor drivers

The muscle. Translates the Pi's commands into wheel power a thousand times a second, through two TB9051FTG driver chips.

🛞

Wheel encoders

The inner ear. Each wheel reports its exact rotation, which powers the smooth control, the self-learning, and the way-back-home memory.

🛡️

Safety is wired in, not bolted on. If the brain ever goes quiet — a crash, an unplugged cable, anything — the motors stop themselves within half a second. And when someone steps into the cart's path, forward motion pauses until they're clear.
